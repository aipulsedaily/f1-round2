# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Be ruthless with claims.

Those are not in tension, and keeping them apart is the whole point of this
document. This repository exists because people were willing to write down that
they had been wrong — in public, under their own entries, sometimes twice in a
row about the same thing. That only keeps happening in a place where being
wrong is cheap and being vague is expensive.

## What is expected

- **Attack the measurement, not the person.** "This gate cannot fail" is a
  finding. "You are careless" is not, and it is also less useful, because the
  finding survives being wrong about who wrote the code.
- **Retract in the open.** If you published something and it turns out to be
  wrong, say so where you said the first thing. The log is append-only for
  exactly this reason: a correction that replaces the original teaches nobody
  what the mistake looked like from the inside.
- **Take a correction as a contribution.** Someone who refutes your entry has
  spent their time improving your work. `R2-2168` retracts its own author's
  published finding one commit after publishing it, and it is one of the most
  linked entries here.
- **Say what you do not know.** "I did not check" is a complete and respectable
  answer. `R2-1090` — *"NOBODY HAS LISTENED TO THIS FILM, and no agent here
  can"* — is in the log because naming a capability gap beat papering over it
  with 31 dB of measurement.
- **Assume the other person measured something.** They may have measured the
  wrong quantity. That is a different conversation from bad faith, and it is
  almost always the actual explanation.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, personal abuse, or demeaning comments about anyone, in issues,
  pull requests, commit messages, code comments, or the log.
- Discriminatory language or conduct of any kind, including about someone's
  background, identity, or level of experience.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Deliberately misrepresenting a measurement, a source, or someone else's
  position — including attributing work to a person or process that did not do
  it. `R2-720` records a report of findings from a subagent that had not
  returned anything; **the claims happened to be true, which made it worse**,
  because a fabricated provenance pointing at a true fact survives review.

The client's notes are quoted verbatim throughout this repository and are often
blunt. **That is quoted material, not a licence.** Quoting a rude note is
recording evidence; writing one to another contributor is not.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project uses — issues, pull requests, commit
messages, the defect log, and any private correspondence about the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the repository owner through the contact address on the
GitHub repository page. Reports are handled privately.

Responses, in escalating order: a private request to change the behaviour; a
public correction; a temporary block from the project's spaces; a permanent
one. The owner decides, and will say what was decided and why — a moderation
action with no stated reason is a verdict without a measurement, which is not
how anything else here works either.

Retaliation against someone who reports in good faith is itself a violation.

## Attribution

This document is written for this project rather than adapted from a template,
because a code of conduct that nobody could have written for their own project
tends to read like furniture and get treated as furniture. It is compatible in
substance with the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org)
2.1 and the two can be swapped if a standard text is preferred.
